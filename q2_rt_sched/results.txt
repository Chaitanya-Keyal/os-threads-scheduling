Observations
-------------------------------------------------
Program A (CPU-bound)
- Involuntary context switches fall as the quantum grows (about 6950 at 1ms,
  720 at 10ms, 160 at 50ms, about 35 at 300-500ms). Each child needs about
  1 s of CPU, so the total is roughly (total CPU time / quantum). The 30-ish
  left at large quanta and under FIFO come from RT throttling: real-time
  tasks may use 950 ms of every second, and the remaining 50 ms preempts them.
- Voluntary switches stay at 8: a CPU-bound child blocks only once, when it
  writes its report and exits.
- Response time grows with the quantum. The parent forks all 8 children
  before it blocks, so child i first runs after i quanta of its siblings:
  about 30-45ms at 1-10ms, 190ms at 50ms, 600ms at 150ms, 1.2s at 300ms and
  2.6s at 500ms (maximum about 7 quanta).
- Turnaround and throughput barely change (about 8.2-8.8 s, 0.8-0.96
  children/s). The CPU does the same total work whatever the quantum, and
  with a single core no child can finish before the CPU has done nearly all
  of it, because round-robin interleaves all 8. Very small quanta pay a small
  switching cost. Large quanta add a little run-to-run noise.
- SCHED_FIFO runs the children one after another: the worst response time
  (8.7 s, child 8 waits for 7 others) but the best average turnaround
  (5.7 s against about 8.2 s), because early children finish early instead of
  all finishing near the end. Total elapsed time and throughput are the same.

Program B (I/O-bound)
- About 55,000 voluntary switches in every run: each of the 16,000
  write+fsync pairs blocks on the disk (several times per fsync). The
  children give up the CPU long before any quantum expires.
- Involuntary switches are low and only noticeable at 1ms (about 1000), where
  a child is occasionally preempted between finishing one fsync and blocking
  on the next.
- Response time is a few ms for every quantum and under FIFO: the children
  block almost immediately, so each new child gets the CPU at once.
- Turnaround (15-18 s) and throughput (0.44-0.54 children/s) are set by the
  disk, not by the scheduler, so neither the quantum nor FIFO vs RR changes
  them meaningfully. The variation between runs is disk and host noise.

Notes
- The quantum is counted in scheduler ticks of CPU time used by the task.
  With HZ=1000 every value is exact, but wall-clock quanta inside the VM are
  somewhat longer than nominal because the host occasionally deschedules the
  virtual CPU (steal time) and because of RT throttling.
